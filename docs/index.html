Features

Three things, no ceremony.

Every shot lands in an overlay first. Nothing hits your desktop until you say so.

Capture, then choose

Area, window or full screen, with global shortcuts and the native macOS selection. The overlay lets you copy without saving, save to the desktop, pin it on top, or drag it straight into Finder or any app.

Text from anywhere

Select a region and the text inside it lands in your clipboard. French and English, powered by Apple's Vision framework, fully offline.

Annotate and export

Arrow, rectangle, highlight, text and pixelated blur. Exported at the original resolution, every time.

Install

Two minutes, one permission.

The build is not notarized, so macOS asks twice. Once you're through, it just works.

Or build it yourself. Swift Package Manager, no Xcode project.

# installs Snappy.app into /Applications
bash scripts/build-app.sh
1

Download the DMG

Grab the latest release and drag Snappy.app into /Applications.

2

Open it once, the hard way

On first launch macOS refuses it. Go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Right-click › Open no longer works on macOS 15.

3

Allow screen recording

Accept the Screen Recording prompt, then quit and relaunch Snappy. It can't capture anything before that.

Shortcuts

Three shortcuts to remember.

All of them are yours to change in Settings.

Capture an area or a window⌘⇧1
Capture the whole screen⌘⇧2
Copy text from a region (OCR)⌘⇧0

Use as default, in Settings, swaps macOS's own ⌘⇧3 and ⌘⇧4 for Snappy.

Take the shot. Keep your desktop.
